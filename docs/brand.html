Firemark brand sheet

Everything needed to keep Firemark looking and sounding the same everywhere: logo files, colors, type, voice and a few rules.

Logo

One shape that reads two ways: a flame, and a map pin pointing at your spot. The charcoal body is the old fire mark, and the orange-and-gold fire is lit inside it. Use the charcoal logo on light backgrounds and the paper one on charcoal. The one-color versions have the fire cut out, so they work for single-color print, a sign or a truck decal. Below 32 pixels, use the charcoal app tile instead of the bare pin.

Using it

  • Leave clear space around the logo at least as wide as the mark.
  • Keep the full logo at least 120 pixels wide on screens and 1 inch wide in print. Below that, use the symbol.
  • Don’t recolor the fire, stretch the logo, or add shadows and outlines.
  • Don’t put the full-color logo on a photo. Use the white one.

Color

Light stone and charcoal, with orange used sparingly: the logo’s fire, the map pin, the main buttons (in Ember), link underlines, the focus ring and a few icons. Flame orange is never used for small text. Use Ember for that.

Type

One family, Bricolage Grotesque, from Google Fonts (free, open license). Headings are heavy and tight; body text is regular and roomy. Sentence case everywhere, never all caps.

Voice

A sharp neighbor who’s great at this. Plain English, short sentences, talking to “you.” The brand speaks first: “Firemark builds…”, never “we.” Jeremy appears by first name only.

Icons, shapes and motion

  • Icons come from Phosphor (regular weight, free, open license), drawn in charcoal, with Ember for ticks and a few accents.
  • Two shapes only: buttons and chips are fully round, like the pins and chips on a map. Pictures, the form and panels get one small 8 pixel corner.
  • One big moving moment, the homepage map: the search types in, the pin drops and the listing appears. Everything else only rises gently into place once. Anyone who turns on reduced motion sees the finished page right away.
